H1
How to Identify Your Window Balance

Intro
Window won’t stay up? Drops suddenly? Hard to lift?
You’re almost certainly dealing with a worn or broken window balance.
This guide shows you exactly how to identify the correct replacement — even if your balance has no label or part number.

Step 1 — Remove the Balance From the Window
You cannot identify a balance while it’s still installed.

You’ll need:

Flat screwdriver

Phillips screwdriver

Gloves (recommended)

Remove the sash, then remove the balance from the track.

Step 2 — Identify Your Balance Type
There are four main balance types:

1. Channel Balance (Block & Tackle)
Metal U‑shaped channel

Nylon top and bottom fittings

Common in vinyl and aluminum windows

2. Spiral Balance
Long metal tube

Spiral rod at the end

Common in older wood windows

3. Constant Force Coil Balance
Flat stainless steel coil

Mounted in a plastic housing

Common in modern vinyl windows

4. Hybrid / Specialty Balances
Twin coil assemblies

Block & tackle with integrated shoes

Brand‑specific systems

Step 3 — Measure Your Balance Correctly
Channel Balance
Measure metal channel only (not the fittings)

Example: 28" channel → order “28” series”

Spiral Balance
Measure tube length only

Identify color tip (red, blue, green, black)

Coil Balance
Look for stamped numbers on the coil (e.g., “5”, “5.5”, “6”)

Count the number of coils in the assembly

Step 4 — Identify Your Fittings (Critical)
For channel balances, the top and bottom nylon fittings determine the correct replacement.

Look for:

Winged vs. non‑winged

Hook vs. clip

Shoe width (typically 1", 1‑1/4", or 1‑3/8")

Step 5 — Look for Stamped Numbers
Common stamps include:

2830

2740

2950

3850

These indicate the weight range, not the length.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
Measuring the entire balance including fittings

Ordering by window size instead of balance size

Ignoring the shoe width

Assuming all balances are interchangeable
