1. Information architecture and navigation
Homepage focus is strong, but depth is buried:  
Issue: Great positioning (“35,000+ parts”, veteran‑owned, free Parts ID), but deeper categories and education live mostly below the fold or on separate Group pages.
Fix:

Add top‑level nav buckets: “Window Parts”, “Door Parts”, “Shop by Brand”, “Shop by Problem”, “Free Parts ID”.

Expose key categories in mega‑menu: balances, operators, rollers, locks, weatherstripping, screens.

No cross‑site cohesion with Group catalog:  
Issue: The Group site has massive brand/category depth that isn’t clearly surfaced from the main domain.
Fix:

Add a “Legacy & OEM Catalog” link pointing into the Group catalog.

Standardize naming (e.g., “Truth/EntryGard”, “Andersen”, “Pella”) across both sites.

2. Search, discovery, and “Shop by Problem”
“Shop by Problem” is excellent but under‑leveraged:  
Issue: The problem‑based tiles are clear (window won’t stay up, patio door hard to slide, etc.), but they likely just link to generic categories.
Fix:

Route each tile to pre‑filtered category pages (e.g., “Window Won’t Stay Up → balances only, sorted by popularity”).

Add short explainer text at top of each problem page: “If your window drops, you almost always need a balance.”

No advanced search / filters visible on main site:  
Issue: For a 35k‑part catalog, basic search is not enough.
Fix:

Implement filters: brand, series/model, material, color, handing, length/size, price, in‑stock.

Add search suggestions: “Truth EntryGard operator”, “Pella casement crank”, “patio door roller”.

3. SEO and content
Homepage copy is strong but repetitive and slightly messy:  
Issue: Phrases like “35,000+ parts” and “hard‑to‑find” are repeated heavily; earlier you had duplicate paragraphs and awkward commas—those must stay cleaned up.
Fix:

Keep one strong hero paragraph; vary supporting language (“largest catalog of OEM and obsolete parts”, “legacy and discontinued hardware experts”).

Ensure no duplicate blocks or repeated testimonials render again.

Category and product SEO underutilized:  
Issue: Group catalog pages show long lists with thin or generic descriptions and many $0.00 or placeholder prices.
Fix:

For each major category (balances, operators, rollers, weatherstripping, locks), add:

Intro paragraph: what problems it solves, how to identify the part.

FAQ block: “How do I measure?”, “What if it doesn’t fit?”, “Can you cross‑reference my old part number?”

For products, use your Truth/EntryGard‑style template: symptoms, fitment, measurement, install notes, cross‑refs.

Schema markup missing or minimal:  
Issue: No visible JSON‑LD for Product, Organization, Breadcrumb, or FAQ on the main site.
Fix:

Add Organization schema on all pages.

Add Product schema on product pages (price, availability, SKU, brand).

Add BreadcrumbList on category/product pages.

Add FAQPage schema to “Shop by Problem” and key category pages.

4. Product data and pricing
$0.00 and token prices in Group catalog:  
Issue: Many items show $0.00 or $0.02 etc., clearly placeholders.
Fix:

Hide or mark these as “Special Order / Quote Required” instead of showing near‑zero prices.

Apply your markup logic (highest scraped price + 10%) where you have real cost data.

Ensure WooCommerce (or your store engine) never exposes $0.00 as a normal price.

SKU consistency and structure:  
Issue: Main site SKUs (AWDP‑…) look structured; Group catalog SKUs are mixed.
Fix:

Enforce your PROFITABLE cipher/encoding for all new SKUs.

Add a SKU validator in admin and bulk editor.

Map legacy SKUs to new AWDP SKUs via a cross‑reference table.

Variant handling vs duplicates:  
Issue: With so many brands and sizes, you’re at high risk of duplicate listings instead of variants.
Fix:

Normalize into single products with variants (color, handing, length, finish).

Use your hybrid matching logic to merge near‑duplicates.

5. Performance and Core Web Vitals
Heavy imagery and no visible lazy loading:  
Issue: Homepage and catalog rely on multiple product images and hero imagery; likely not all are lazy‑loaded or optimized.
Fix:

Convert product and hero images to WebP (and AVIF where possible).

Add loading="lazy" to all non‑critical images.

Use srcset and sizes for responsive delivery.

Potential render‑blocking scripts and CSS:  
Issue: Typical Woo/WordPress setups ship multiple JS/CSS bundles that hurt LCP and FID.
Fix:

Defer non‑critical JS; inline only what’s needed for above‑the‑fold.

Purge unused CSS (Tailwind purge or CSSO/other minifier).

Preload hero image and primary font.

6. Trust, conversion, and UX
Strong trust story, but not fully leveraged in checkout:  
Issue: Veteran‑owned, 40+ years, free Parts ID are prominent on homepage but not guaranteed to be visible in cart/checkout.
Fix:

Add a “Why buy from us?” strip in cart/checkout: veteran‑owned, expert ID, obsolete parts, phone support.

Add badges: secure checkout, fast shipping, free Parts ID.

Shipping and returns clarity:  
Issue: There’s a note about orders under $50 possibly needing extra shipping, but it’s easy to miss.
Fix:

Show a shipping cost estimate or clear policy near “Add to Cart”.

Add a short FAQ: “What if the part doesn’t fit?”, “Do you accept returns on special orders?”

Image branding and consistency:  
Issue: Product images don’t consistently show your full‑color logo overlay and aspect ratios vary.
Fix:

Batch‑process images with your full‑color overlay (no watermarking/dimming).

Standardize to a single aspect ratio (1:1 or 4:5) for all catalog images.

7. Technical/admin
Bulk management is essential at your scale:  
Issue: With tens of thousands of SKUs (especially in the Group catalog), manual edits are not sustainable.
Fix:

Ensure your bulk editor can handle: price, category, attributes, visibility, SKU validation, image validation.

Add logging for: upload failures, invalid SKUs, missing required attributes.

Error handling and stability:  
Issue: Historically you’ve had crashes around image upload and admin actions; at this scale, silent failures are expensive.
Fix:

Wrap admin and critical customer flows in error boundaries.

Log stack traces server‑side with clear tags (UPLOAD_ERROR, SKU_ERROR, PRICE_ERROR).

8. Priority roadmap (from this audit)
Clean product data: remove/convert $0.00 and token prices; enforce SKU rules; merge duplicates.

Upgrade discovery: filters, better search, deeper “Shop by Problem” routing.

Implement schema + metadata: Product, Organization, Breadcrumb, FAQ; tighten titles/descriptions.

Optimize performance: image optimization, lazy loading, JS/CSS deferral, LCP/CLS fixes.

Strengthen conversion paths: trust badges, shipping clarity, checkout reassurance.

Unify main + Group catalogs: clear links, consistent naming, cross‑reference mapping.
