H1
How to Identify Your Weatherstripping

Intro
Drafts, rattling, or visible daylight around your window or door usually means the weatherstripping has worn out.
This guide shows you how to identify the correct replacement — even if the original seal is flattened, brittle, or missing sections.

Step 1 — Remove a Clean Sample
Cut a 1–2 inch piece from an undamaged section.

Avoid crushed or flattened areas — they distort the profile.

Step 2 — Identify the Profile Type
Most weatherstripping falls into one of these categories:

1. Kerf-In Weatherstripping
Has a T-shaped barb

Press‑fits into a kerf slot

Common on vinyl and wood windows

2. Bulb Seal
Round or teardrop‑shaped bulb

Used on doors and casement windows

3. Foam Seal
Soft, compressible

Often adhesive‑backed

Used on older windows and storm panels

4. Fin Seal (Pile Weatherstripping)
Fuzzy pile with a center fin

Used on sliding windows and patio doors

5. OEM‑Specific Profiles
Unique shapes for Andersen, Pella, Milgard, etc.

Step 3 — Measure the Critical Dimensions
Kerf Weatherstripping
Kerf width (usually 1/8" or 3/16")

Bulb diameter

Overall height

Bulb Seal
Bulb diameter

Base width

Stem height

Fin Seal
Pile height (1/4", 5/16", 3/8")

Base width

Fin height

Step 4 — Match the Profile Shape
Lay the sample flat and compare the silhouette to:

OEM diagrams

Product photos

Cross‑section drawings

Shape is more important than color.

Common Mistakes
Measuring flattened or worn sections

Ordering by door/window brand alone

Ignoring kerf width

Assuming “close enough” will seal properly
